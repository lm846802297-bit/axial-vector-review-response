# Fig. 7 vector luminosity, 10 MeV: normalization-free coupling-shape audit

This audit separates a mismatch in the **shape of the strong-coupling decline** from a constant luminosity normalization mismatch. It uses the exact 25 common visible vertices of the published Fig. 7 10 MeV vector curve and the existing high-order B.16 calculation. It changes no production, opacity, profile, workflow, or bound. The reference is the plotted centerline, not the authors' tabulated luminosity or raw transport.

## Derivation

For a fixed domain with nonnegative coupling-independent source weights and ray optical depths that scale as \(g^2\), set \(x=(g/g_*)^2\) and let \(\tau_*\) denote the optical depth at \(g_*\). Up to fixed units,
$$
Q(x)=\frac{L(g)}{g^2}=\int w(d\tau_*)\,e^{-x\tau_*},
$$
$$
-\frac{d\log Q}{dx}=\langle\tau_*\rangle_x,\qquad
\frac{d^2\log Q}{dx^2}=\operatorname{Var}_x(\tau_*)\geq0.
$$
A negative secant of $\log Q$ is the interval mean of the escaping-source depth, not an estimate of local opacity. The code checks only the **necessary** decreasing and convex shape on the sampled grid. A convex interpolant does not prove that the curve admits a positive Laplace mixture. The linear program asks for the minimum symmetric vertical envelope about the archived centerline within which these inequalities are feasible at fixed $g$; it neither reports an adjusted reference nor fits a transport model.

## Results

- Geometry: Fig. 7 vector path 43, 25 visible vertices, with maximum relative mismatch $1.11\times10^{-16}$ between the independent coordinate conversion and the archived luminosity/coupling arrays. The same $g$ nodes, reference luminosities, and optically thin values are used for strict B.16 and the conserved-energy path redshift-only diagnostic. In both, the archived optically thin luminosity is proportional to $g^2$.
- Shape: neither calculation violates the necessary shape at any node. The published centerline is decreasing after dividing by $g^2$; five pre-peak convexity inequalities have small positive defects (maximum 0.008488 in natural log), and none from the reference peak onward. A simultaneous minimum envelope of ±0.111406 PDF points is sufficient for the inequalities at fixed $g$, less than the ±0.75 PDF-point nominal half stroke width. This is a scale comparison in the vertical coordinate, not a statistical uncertainty, full stroke geometry, or proof that the numerical reference lies inside that band.
- From the published peak vertex $g=1.13411087\times10^{-8}$ to the last visible $g=4.17168965\times10^{-8}$, the strict calculated/published ratio grows from **1.02316 to 1.92428**, a factor of **1.88073**. The redshift-only ratio grows from **0.96980 to 1.36913**, a factor of **1.41177**. The corresponding log ratio changes are 0.63166 and 0.34484, versus 0.05933 from allowing opposite vertical shifts of half the nominal stroke width at the two fixed couplings.
- The last visible secant's mean escape exponents are **6.30692** (published centerline), **5.77637** (strict), **5.97211** (redshift-only), using $g_*$ equal to the last visible coupling. These numbers are not physical opacities and do not apply beyond the last vertex.

Subtracting the log ratio at the reference peak eliminates any constant source normalization algebraically. Thus a constant normalization change alone cannot reconcile the sampled coupling dependence. The diagnostic does **not** distinguish a changed depth distribution from changed source spectral or spatial weights, moving domains, optical-depth conventions, or other changes to the reference model. Redshift-only here is not a full GR calculation. The strict 10 MeV last-vertex $+92.4284\%$ residual is unchanged. No fit and no rescaling were used, and this audit does not establish a new luminosity or constraint.
