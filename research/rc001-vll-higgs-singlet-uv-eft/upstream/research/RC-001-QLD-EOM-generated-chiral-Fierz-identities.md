# RC-001 EOM-generated chiral Fierz identities and evanescent differences

**Date:** 2026-11-10  
**Scope:** four-dimensional Fierz projections required after the second-order fermion EOM generates scalar–vector and scalar–tensor spinor products. Exact component checks are provided. This is not a complete one-loop evanescent mixing matrix.

## 1. Conventions and crossed spinor pairing

Use
\[
P_{R,L}=\frac{1\pm\gamma_5}{2},\qquad
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{2}[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu],\qquad
g_{\mu\nu}=(+---),\qquad \epsilon^{0123}=+1.
\]
For c-number spinor matrices, define the crossed pairing by
\[
(A\widetilde\otimes B)_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
\equiv A_{\alpha\delta}B_{\gamma\beta},
\]
whereas the original pairing is
\[
(A\otimes B)_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
\equiv A_{\alpha\beta}B_{\gamma\delta}.
\]
The identities below are c-number matrix identities. When translated into four-fermion operator identities, exchanging the middle two anticommuting fermion fields supplies an additional overall minus sign.

## 2. Scalar–vector Fierz identities

The exact four-dimensional identities are
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_R\otimes\gamma^\mu P_R
={}&\frac12P_R\widetilde\otimes\gamma^\mu P_R
+\frac{i}{2}\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_R
 \widetilde\otimes\gamma_\nu P_R,\\
P_R\otimes\gamma^\mu P_L
={}&\frac12\gamma^\mu P_L\widetilde\otimes P_R
-\frac{i}{2}\gamma_\nu P_L
 \widetilde\otimes\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_R,\\
P_L\otimes\gamma^\mu P_R
={}&\frac12\gamma^\mu P_R\widetilde\otimes P_L
-\frac{i}{2}\gamma_\nu P_R
 \widetilde\otimes\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_L,\\
P_L\otimes\gamma^\mu P_L
={}&\frac12P_L\widetilde\otimes\gamma^\mu P_L
+\frac{i}{2}\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_L
 \widetilde\otimes\gamma_\nu P_L.
\end{aligned}
\]
For example, the corresponding operator identity in the first channel is
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\bar\psi P_R\chi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\mu P_R\xi)
={}&-\frac12(\bar\psi P_R\xi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\mu P_R\chi)\\
&-\frac{i}{2}(\bar\psi\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_R\xi)
(\bar\eta\gamma_\nu P_R\chi).
\end{aligned}
\]
These identities are needed when the second-order EOM produces terms proportional to \(\gamma^\mu D_\mu H\).

## 3. Scalar–tensor Fierz identities with opposite chiralities

For opposite chiralities,
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_R\otimes\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_L
={}&-\frac{i}{2}\left[
\gamma^\mu P_L\widetilde\otimes\gamma^\nu P_R
-\gamma^\nu P_L\widetilde\otimes\gamma^\mu P_R
\right]\\
&-\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
\gamma_\rho P_L\widetilde\otimes\gamma_\sigma P_R,\\[1mm]
P_L\otimes\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_R
={}&-\frac{i}{2}\left[
\gamma^\mu P_R\widetilde\otimes\gamma^\nu P_L
-\gamma^\nu P_R\widetilde\otimes\gamma^\mu P_L
\right]\\
&+\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
\gamma_\rho P_R\widetilde\otimes\gamma_\sigma P_L.
\end{aligned}
\]
The Levi-Civita terms are essential. Dropping them does not give a valid four-dimensional Fierz identity. These channels arise when a curvature term \(\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mathscr F_{\mu\nu}\) multiplies a scalar source bilinear.

## 4. Scalar–tensor Fierz identities with equal chiralities

A chiral tensor has only three independent components. Define
\[
\Sigma_i^R=\sigma^{0i}P_R,\qquad
\Sigma_i^L=\sigma^{0i}P_L,\qquad i=1,2,3,
\]
with \(\epsilon_{123}=+1\). The self-duality relations in the stated convention are
\[
\sigma^{ij}P_R=-i\epsilon^{ijk}\Sigma_k^R,\qquad
\sigma^{ij}P_L=+i\epsilon^{ijk}\Sigma_k^L.
\]
The exact scalar–tensor Fierz identities can therefore be written in the three-component chiral basis:
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_R\otimes\Sigma_i^R
={}&\frac12P_R\widetilde\otimes\Sigma_i^R
+\frac12\Sigma_i^R\widetilde\otimes P_R
+\frac12\epsilon_{ijk}\Sigma_j^R\widetilde\otimes\Sigma_k^R,\\
P_L\otimes\Sigma_i^L
={}&\frac12P_L\widetilde\otimes\Sigma_i^L
+\frac12\Sigma_i^L\widetilde\otimes P_L
-\frac12\epsilon_{ijk}\Sigma_j^L\widetilde\otimes\Sigma_k^L.
\end{aligned}
\]
This three-component form avoids treating the six \(\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_{R,L}\) components as independent when they are related by chirality/self-duality.

## 5. Evanescent differences for the EOM-generated structures

Let \(\mathcal O_{\Gamma_1\Gamma_2}^{(r,s)}\) denote an operator product with the indicated original spinor matrices and derivative placement labels \(r,s\). Let \(\mathcal F_{\Gamma_1\Gamma_2,\,4D}^{(r,s)}\) be its four-dimensional **operator-level** Fierz projection, including the Grassmann minus sign and carrying every derivative, color, weak, flavor, Higgs, and field-strength label with its original fermion field. Define
\[
\boxed{
E_{\Gamma_1\Gamma_2}^{(r,s)}
=\mathcal O_{\Gamma_1\Gamma_2}^{(r,s)}
-\mathcal F_{\Gamma_1\Gamma_2,\,4D}^{(r,s)}.
}
\]
These differences vanish in exactly four dimensions but are retained in \(D=4-2\epsilon\). They extend the scalar–scalar \(E_{RR}^{(0)},E_{LL}^{(0)},E_{RL}^{(0)},E_{LR}^{(0)}\) definitions to the scalar–vector and scalar–tensor structures generated by the second-order EOM.

### 5.1 Explicit minimal evanescent definitions

For a generic original spinor ordering \((\bar\psi\,\Gamma_1\chi)(\bar\eta\,\Gamma_2\xi)\), the following definitions use the same derivative labels \(r,s\) on the same original fermion fields. All displayed terms are understood with those labels and with the external Higgs/field-strength tensors contracted as in the EOM-generated operator.

For scalar–vector channels:
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{RR;V}^{\mu,rs}={}&
(\bar\psi P_R\chi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\mu P_R\xi)
+\frac12(\bar\psi P_R\xi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\mu P_R\chi)\\
&+\frac{i}{2}(\bar\psi\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_R\xi)
(\bar\eta\gamma_\nu P_R\chi),\\
E_{RL;V}^{\mu,rs}={}&
(\bar\psi P_R\chi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\mu P_L\xi)
+\frac12(\bar\psi\gamma^\mu P_L\xi)(\bar\eta P_R\chi)\\
&-\frac{i}{2}(\bar\psi\gamma_\nu P_L\xi)
(\bar\eta\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_R\chi),\\
E_{LR;V}^{\mu,rs}={}&
(\bar\psi P_L\chi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\mu P_R\xi)
+\frac12(\bar\psi\gamma^\mu P_R\xi)(\bar\eta P_L\chi)\\
&-\frac{i}{2}(\bar\psi\gamma_\nu P_R\xi)
(\bar\eta\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_L\chi),\\
E_{LL;V}^{\mu,rs}={}&
(\bar\psi P_L\chi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\mu P_L\xi)
+\frac12(\bar\psi P_L\xi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\mu P_L\chi)\\
&+\frac{i}{2}(\bar\psi\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_L\xi)
(\bar\eta\gamma_\nu P_L\chi).
\end{aligned}
\]
For opposite-chirality scalar–tensor channels:
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{RL;T}^{\mu\nu,rs}={}&
(\bar\psi P_R\chi)(\bar\eta\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_L\xi)\\
&-\frac{i}{2}\left[
(\bar\psi\gamma^\mu P_L\xi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\nu P_R\chi)
-(\bar\psi\gamma^\nu P_L\xi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\mu P_R\chi)
\right]\\
&-\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
(\bar\psi\gamma_\rho P_L\xi)(\bar\eta\gamma_\sigma P_R\chi),\\
E_{LR;T}^{\mu\nu,rs}={}&
(\bar\psi P_L\chi)(\bar\eta\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_R\xi)\\
&-\frac{i}{2}\left[
(\bar\psi\gamma^\mu P_R\xi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\nu P_L\chi)
-(\bar\psi\gamma^\nu P_R\xi)(\bar\eta\gamma^\mu P_L\chi)
\right]\\
&+\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
(\bar\psi\gamma_\rho P_R\xi)(\bar\eta\gamma_\sigma P_L\chi).
\end{aligned}
\]
For same-chirality scalar–tensor channels, use the three independent \(\Sigma_i^{R,L}\) components:
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{RR;T}^{i,rs}={}&
(\bar\psi P_R\chi)(\bar\eta\Sigma_i^R\xi)
+\frac12(\bar\psi P_R\xi)(\bar\eta\Sigma_i^R\chi)\\
&+\frac12(\bar\psi\Sigma_i^R\xi)(\bar\eta P_R\chi)
+\frac12\epsilon_{ijk}(\bar\psi\Sigma_j^R\xi)(\bar\eta\Sigma_k^R\chi),\\
E_{LL;T}^{i,rs}={}&
(\bar\psi P_L\chi)(\bar\eta\Sigma_i^L\xi)
+\frac12(\bar\psi P_L\xi)(\bar\eta\Sigma_i^L\chi)\\
&+\frac12(\bar\psi\Sigma_i^L\xi)(\bar\eta P_L\chi)
-\frac12\epsilon_{ijk}(\bar\psi\Sigma_j^L\xi)(\bar\eta\Sigma_k^L\chi).
\end{aligned}
\]
The plus signs in these definitions are fixed by subtracting the **operator-level** Fierz projection, which already includes the Grassmann minus sign. The definitions have no \(O(\epsilon)\) physical-operator admixture at the definition stage. The external derivative, Higgs, and curvature factors are spectators and are not included in the abbreviated spinor notation above.

## 6. Scheme and acceptance boundary

Use the QLD8 open-line working prescription already fixed in the master record: NDR with anticommuting \(\gamma_5\), \(D=4-2\epsilon\), and \(\overline{\mathrm{MS}}\). The Fierz-difference definitions above have no \(O(\epsilon)\) physical-operator admixture at the definition stage.

This closes the **four-dimensional Fierz projections** needed for the displayed EOM-generated Dirac structures and defines the corresponding tree-level evanescent differences. It does not establish the one-loop mixing matrix of these evanescents, prove that the set is closed under renormalization, or compute finite UV-minus-EFT Wilson coefficients. Longer antisymmetrized gamma strings generated by UV poles must be added to the counterterm basis, and the UV and EFT sides must use the same enlarged set.
